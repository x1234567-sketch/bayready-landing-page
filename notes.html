BayReady — CA2 Project Notes

Student concept for Kehoe's Garage, Enniscorthy, County Wexford. This is an educational prototype using fictional garage jobs.

1. Original idea — before AI assistance

Busy car repair businesses will pay for the app because, it saves staff time by updating clients on their car status and it provides an automated way to track parts availability and their arrival estimation, so cars wont be forgotten while waiting for parts.

This was my initial hypothesis, not a proven customer finding. The current prototype does not automatically check parts availability or estimate arrival times.

2. Target customer and problem

Target niche: small independent Irish car repair garages with 3–6 mechanics and one office administrator.

The intended buyer is the garage owner or manager. Mechanics need quick status updates; office staff need an overview of jobs; customers want to know their car's repair stage.

The existing process may involve paper job cards, verbal updates, phone calls, and manually checking which cars are waiting for parts. These are assumptions to validate with real garages.

Technology category: a simple web-based garage job-status tracking application.

3. Competitor research

  • Garage Hive: https://garagehive.ie/
  • TechMan: https://www.techmangms.com/

These are existing garage-management software alternatives to investigate. BayReady focuses on a smaller status-tracking prototype rather than claiming to replace a full garage-management system.

Research date: 19 October 2026. These links are recorded as research references; detailed competitor feature and price comparisons have not been independently verified here.

4. Assumed pricing

€99 one-time setup + €29 per month.

This is a proposed price for testing the business idea, not a real offer or validated willingness to pay.

5. Lean product requirements

  1. Workshop: show at least eight clearly fictional sample jobs with large tap buttons.
  2. Workshop: move each job through five repair stages without typing.
  3. Customer: look up only the matching sample car by job number or registration.
  4. Office: group jobs by stage, flag jobs waiting for parts over two days, and count cars ready to collect.
  5. Store demo changes locally in the browser and publish a phone-friendly web prototype.

6. Alpha prototype

Open the working Kehoe's Garage demo

The demo includes Workshop, Customer, and Office screens. It uses fictional jobs and local browser storage, not a shared live garage database.

7. Testing completed

Manual browser and mobile testing, 19 October 2026.

  • Published GitHub Pages demo opened successfully.
  • Workshop displayed fictional sample jobs.
  • Workshop repair-stage buttons changed job status.
  • Job status remained saved after a refresh.
  • Customer lookup worked using only job number KG101.
  • Customer lookup also worked using registration only.
  • Customer lookup showed the matching car only.
  • Office grouped jobs by repair stage.
  • Office showed a red warning for a job waiting more than two days for parts.
  • Ready-to-collect count matched the listed jobs.
  • Website opened and fitted a phone screen.
  • Workshop button was easy to tap on a phone and changed the job status.

8. Problem, change and retest

Problem: Customer search initially required both the job number and registration.

Change: Updated the search logic so either field could identify a sample job. Uploaded the revised index.html to GitHub.

Retest: Searching with KG101 alone worked. Searching with a registration alone also worked.

9. Limitations and next steps

  • Fictional jobs only; no real customer data.
  • Browser-local storage; no cross-device syncing.
  • No automatic parts availability or ETA tracking.
  • No automatic customer notifications.
  • Pricing and customer demand are assumptions.

Next steps would be to interview garage owners, test the workflow with real staff, and validate whether garages would pay for the service.
